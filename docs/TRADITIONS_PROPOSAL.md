# Proposal: the starting set of traditions (for Dave to approve)

**Status: proposal only.** These traditions are stored in the database marked "proposed." No tradition-specific content (lesson versions, "Where Christians differ" sections, perspective summaries) will be written until you approve or change this set.

## Proposed set (five)

| # | Tradition | Who it covers | Where it usually stands on the contested points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Baptist** | Southern Baptist, independent Baptist, Reformed Baptist, and similar | Baptism of believers only, by immersion; the Lord's Supper as a memorial; mostly "once saved, always saved" |
| 2 | **Methodist and Wesleyan** | United Methodist, Wesleyan, Nazarene, Holiness churches | Arminian on election (God chooses on foreseen faith); a believer can fall away; holiness and Christian perfection |
| 3 | **Presbyterian and Reformed** | PCA, PC(USA), Reformed churches | Calvinist on election; infant baptism; the perseverance of the saints |
| 4 | **Anglican and Episcopal** | Episcopal Church, Anglican churches | Infant baptism; liturgy and bishops; historically a broad range on election |
| 5 | **Pentecostal and Charismatic** | Assemblies of God, Church of God, charismatic congregations | Baptism in the Holy Spirit; spiritual gifts today, including tongues and healing |

**Why these five:** together they cover most of the Protestant churches where lay Sunday School teachers and small-group leaders are found. Four of them already have a voice in the library: Henry, Barnes, Jamieson, Brown, Easton and Orr are Presbyterian; Clarke and Bounds are Methodist; Ryle and Fausset are Anglican; Spurgeon is Baptist.

**The catch with Pentecostal and Charismatic:** that movement began in 1901, so it has **no public-domain author in the library yet**. Its views would be written as fair summaries in my own words, with Scripture. Quotations would come only if we later find public-domain early Pentecostal writers. A tradition with no commentator of its own is a real gap, so I'm flagging it plainly.

### Alternatives you might prefer to swap in

- **Roman Catholic.** This is a large audience. Public-domain sources exist and could be added: the Douay-Rheims Bible and Haydock's commentary (1859). The modern Catechism is copyrighted, so I would only summarize it in my own words.
- **Lutheran.** Public-domain sources exist, such as Luther's commentary on Galatians in older translations and the Book of Concord in 19th-century translations. The library has no Lutheran voice yet.
- **Nondenominational evangelical.** Many teachers belong to these churches, and Moody and Torrey fit here. The difficulty is that these churches don't share one position on the contested points. Most look Baptist on baptism, and many teach a premillennial view of the end times. Because there is no single position to present, I would rather not make this a filter tradition.

Two things to know:

- **JFB has three authors from two traditions.** On the Old Testament prophets the voice is Fausset's (Anglican). On the New Testament it is Brown's (Presbyterian).

## Proposed contested topics (please confirm or edit)

These Nave's topics would be marked "contested." A lesson on any of them gets the "Where Christians differ" section and one version per approved tradition. Every other topic is treated as one where Christians broadly agree.

| Topic | What Christians disagree about |
|---|---|
| Baptism | Who (believers only, or infants too), how (immersion, pouring, sprinkling), and what it does |
| Lord's Supper | What the bread and cup are (memorial, spiritual presence, real presence), and who may take them |
| Predestination; Election | Whether God chooses unconditionally (Reformed) or on foreseen faith (Wesleyan) |
| Perseverance; Apostasy | Whether a true believer can finally fall away |
| Perfection; Sanctification | Whether Christians can reach "entire sanctification" in this life |
| Millennium; Antichrist | Premillennial, postmillennial and amillennial readings of the end times |
| Tongues (the Gift) | Whether tongues continue today, and whether they mark baptism in the Spirit |
| Sabbath; Lord's Day | Whether the Sabbath command carries over to Sunday, and how strictly |

Some disagreements sit inside topics that are otherwise uncontested, for example women in church leadership within "Women", and church government within "Elder" and "Bishop". I've left those topics unmarked. If a lesson on one of them reaches the disputed point, that lesson would add a short "Where Christians differ" note.
