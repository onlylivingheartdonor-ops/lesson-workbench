== opening | 4
Ask: "If you suddenly received a large inheritance, what is the first thing you would do with it?" Let people answer honestly and with humour. Then ask: "How do you think it would change you?" Today Jesus tells a story about a man who had a very good year.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. Ask the class to count how many times the rich man says "I" or "my."

== background | 5
A man in the crowd asked Jesus to settle a family dispute over an inheritance. Rabbis were sometimes asked to rule on such questions. Jesus refused to act as judge (v. 14); instead he went to the heart beneath the dispute: greed. "Covetousness" means wanting more, especially what is not ours to have.

The rich man in the parable did nothing illegal. His land produced a bumper crop. His problem was what he planned to do with it, and whom he left out of his plans.

== point | 7 | Life is more than possessions
"Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth" (v. 15). Jesus warns against "all" covetousness, every kind. Our culture constantly tells us that the next purchase will satisfy. Jesus says that is a lie. Things cannot fill a soul made for God.

== point | 7 | The rich man's plans left God out
In verses 17-19 the rich man talks only to himself. "I" and "my" fill his speech: my fruits, my barns, my goods, my soul. He plans to build bigger barns and then retire to ease. There is no thought of God, no thought of others, no thought of death. JFB lists the reasons Jesus calls him a fool, beginning with this one:

== point | 7 | Be rich toward God
"Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee." All his planning came to nothing in one night. Then Jesus applies the story: "So is he that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God" (v. 21). Being rich toward God means investing our lives and resources in what he values: him, his kingdom, and people in need.

== questions | 7
q: Was it wrong for the man to have a good harvest, or to build barns? What was wrong? | No; the crop was a gift. What was wrong was his self-centred plans, his forgetting of God and others, and his false sense of security.
q: How does our culture encourage covetousness? | Advertising, comparison, social media, the idea that more brings security and happiness.
q: What would it look like in practice to be "rich toward God"? | Generosity, prayerful use of money, investing time in God's kingdom, caring for the poor, contentment.
q: How does remembering that life is short help us fight greed? | It puts possessions in perspective; we cannot take them with us.

== application | 3
Take an honest look at your spending and giving this week. Choose one specific way to be "rich toward God": increase a gift, help someone in need, or give away something you do not use. Pray 1 Timothy 6:6: "godliness with contentment is great gain."

== prayer | 2
Lord, everything we have comes from you. Forgive us for trusting in things and for planning our lives without you. Free us from every kind of covetousness. Teach us that life is more than possessions, and make us rich toward you and generous toward others. In Jesus' name, Amen.
