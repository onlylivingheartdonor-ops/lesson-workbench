title: Faithful in Everything
big_idea: Like Daniel, God's people are called to be so faithful at work and so steady in prayer that the only charge against them concerns their devotion to God.

== opening | 4
Ask: "If someone went looking for dirt on you at work, at home, or in your finances, what would they find?" Give the class a moment to think privately. Then say: "Daniel's enemies did exactly that, and they came up empty." Most people know the end of this story, the lions' den. Today we look at what came before it.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. If there is time, read the rest of the chapter (vv. 11-28) so the class hears how the story ends.

== background | 5
Daniel had been taken to Babylon as a young man about seventy years earlier (Daniel 1). He served under Babylonian kings, and now, in old age, under Darius the Mede after Babylon fell to the Medes and Persians. The king planned to set him over the whole kingdom, which made the other officials jealous.

The laws of the Medes and Persians, once signed, could not be changed (v. 8). Daniel's enemies used the king's pride to trap him: a thirty-day ban on praying to anyone but the king.
note: Daniel prayed toward Jerusalem because of Solomon's prayer at the temple's dedication (1 Kings 8:46-49), which asked God to hear his people when they prayed toward the city from exile.

== point | 7 | Faithful at work
Daniel had "an excellent spirit" (v. 3). He was so faithful and capable that the king wanted to promote him over everyone. When his rivals searched his record, they found "no error or fault" (v. 4). Integrity starts with ordinary faithfulness in daily work, done well and honestly, because we serve God in it.
verses: Daniel 6:1-4; Colossians 3:23-24; Proverbs 22:29

== point | 7 | Blameless, except for his faith
"We shall not find any occasion against this Daniel, except we find it against him concerning the law of his God" (v. 5). What a reputation! The only thing they could attack was his faithfulness to God. A believer's life should give critics nothing to point to except Christ.
verses: Daniel 6:5-9; 1 Peter 2:12; 1 Peter 3:15-16

== point | 7 | Steady in prayer under pressure
When Daniel knew the law was signed, he went home, opened his windows toward Jerusalem, and prayed three times a day, giving thanks, "as he did aforetime" (v. 10). He neither hid nor made a show of it; he simply kept doing what he had always done. Daniel's faithfulness in the crisis grew out of years of faithful habits.
verses: Daniel 6:10; Psalm 55:17; Acts 5:29

note: Daniel did not rebel against the king in other matters; he served him loyally. He disobeyed only the one law that forbade him to pray to God. Christians agree that when a human law requires disobeying God, "we ought to obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29).

== questions | 7
q: What does Daniel's work record teach us about how Christians should do their jobs? | Excellence, honesty and reliability are part of serving God; our work is a witness.
q: What would it mean for your reputation to be "blameless except for your faith"? | Answers will vary; others should see integrity in money, speech, relationships and work.
q: Why do you think Daniel did not close his windows or change his routine? | He would not hide his faith or bargain with it; his prayer habit was settled long before the crisis.
q: What habits are you building now that will hold you steady in a crisis? | Daily prayer, Scripture reading, worship, accountability.

== application | 3
Choose one area of your work or daily responsibilities where you can be more faithful this week, even when no one is watching. Set a regular time and place for prayer and keep it every day, as Daniel did.

== prayer | 2
Faithful God, thank you for Daniel's example. Make us faithful in our work and honest in all our dealings, so that our lives point to you. Give us steady habits of prayer, and courage to keep praying and obeying when it costs us something. Keep us faithful to you in everything. In Jesus' name, Amen.
