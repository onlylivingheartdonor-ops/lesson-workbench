== opening | 4
Ask: "What do you trust every day without seeing it?" (A chair before you sit in it, a bridge, the pilot of a plane, a doctor's prescription.) Then ask: "What makes that trust reasonable?" Usually it is the reliability of the one we are trusting. Faith in the Bible works the same way. Its strength depends on whom it trusts.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. As each name comes up (Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham), pause to let the class recall in a phrase what that person did.

== background | 5
Hebrews was written to Jewish Christians who were under pressure and tempted to give up and go back. Chapter 10 ends, "the just shall live by faith ... we are not of them who draw back" (10:38-39). Chapter 11 then explains what that faith looks like, using a long line of Old Testament believers, often called the "hall of faith."

The writer's point is not that these people were perfect; their stories show many failures. The point is that each one trusted God's word and acted on it before seeing the outcome. Chapter 12 then turns to us: "Seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses ... let us run with patience" (12:1).
note: The writer of Hebrews is not named in the letter. Christians have suggested Paul, Apollos, Barnabas and others; the lesson simply says "the writer of Hebrews."

== point | 7 | Faith trusts what God has said about what we cannot see
Verse 1 gives a description of faith: it gives substance to what we hope for and is convinced of what we cannot see. Faith is not wishful thinking. It rests on what God has said. Verse 3 gives the first example. None of us saw the world made, but we believe God made it because he has told us so.
verses: Hebrews 11:1-3; 2 Corinthians 5:7; Romans 10:17

== point | 7 | Without faith it is impossible to please God
Abel, Enoch and Noah pleased God because they trusted him. Verse 6 states the principle: whoever comes to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him. Faith is more than knowing facts about God. It is coming to him and relying on him.
verses: Hebrews 11:4-6; Romans 4:20-21; James 2:19
quote: 52752 | Without confidence in God--in his fidelity, his truth, his wisdom, his promises.
quote: 93735 | But a man may know a thing, and yet not have faith. I may know a thing, and yet not believe it.

note: Spurgeon's sermon on Hebrews 11:6 (in the library) works through knowledge, assent and trust as the parts of faith, if the class wants to go deeper.

== point | 7 | Faith obeys and keeps going
Noah built an ark before a drop of rain fell. Abraham left home "not knowing whither he went" (v. 8). He lived in tents in the very land God had promised him. He kept trusting because he was looking beyond this life to "a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God" (v. 10). Real faith moves our feet, and it keeps moving them when the promise is slow in coming.
verses: Hebrews 11:7-10; Genesis 12:1-4; Hebrews 11:13-16

== questions | 7
q: How would you explain the difference between faith and wishful thinking? | Wishful thinking rests on what we want; faith rests on what God has said and on his proven character.
q: Why do you think the writer chose people whose stories include failures? | To show that faith is trust in God, not human perfection. God works through ordinary, flawed people who rely on him.
q: Noah and Abraham acted before they saw anything happen. Where is God asking you to trust him before you see the outcome? | Answers will vary (a decision, a relationship, giving, a move, an illness).
q: Abraham looked for a heavenly city. How does hope for the future help us keep trusting now? | Knowing God's promises reach beyond this life gives patience when earthly circumstances disappoint.

== application | 3
Write down one promise of God that speaks to a situation you are facing (for example Philippians 4:19, Isaiah 41:10, or Romans 8:28). Read it every morning this week. Then take one concrete step of obedience that shows you are trusting it.

== prayer | 2
Faithful God, you have spoken, and your word does not fail. Strengthen our faith where it is weak. Help us trust you with what we cannot see, obey you when we do not know where the road leads, and keep looking to the city you are preparing. Through Jesus Christ, the author and finisher of our faith, Amen.
