== opening | 4
Hold up (or describe) a plain clay flowerpot. Ask: "Would anyone keep diamonds in this?" Probably not; we put treasure in strong boxes. Yet Paul says God has put the greatest treasure in the world in fragile clay containers: us. Today's lesson is for anyone who feels cracked, tired or under pressure.
note: Some in the class may be grieving or ill. Allow space for honesty; this lesson is not about pretending suffering is small, but about where hope is found in it.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. Pause after verses 8-9 and let the four "yet not" contrasts sink in.

== background | 5
Second Corinthians is Paul's most personal letter. Some in Corinth looked down on him: he was not impressive to look at, he suffered constantly, and his life did not look like success. In chapters 4-6 Paul answers that his weakness is the very place where God's power is seen.

Paul had faced beatings, imprisonments, shipwreck, hunger and danger (11:23-28). He is not speaking from a comfortable chair when he calls his troubles "light affliction" (v. 17). He is weighing them against eternity.

== point | 7 | God puts his treasure in clay jars
The treasure is the gospel: "the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (v. 6). The container is ordinary, breakable people. God does this on purpose, so that it is plain the power is his and not ours. Our weakness is not a barrier to God's work. It is the stage on which his power is shown.
verses: 2 Corinthians 4:6-7; 2 Corinthians 12:9-10; 1 Corinthians 1:27

== point | 7 | Pressed, but not crushed
Paul lists four hardships, and each one has a "but not": troubled but not distressed, perplexed but not in despair, persecuted but not forsaken, cast down but not destroyed. Christians are not spared trouble. But trouble never has the last word, because God never leaves us. Jesus was truly forsaken on the cross so that his people never will be.

== point | 7 | Look at what is unseen and eternal
"For which cause we faint not" (v. 16). Our bodies wear out, but inwardly we are renewed day by day. Paul calls his sufferings light and momentary when set beside an "eternal weight of glory." The key is where we fix our eyes. We look past what is seen and temporary to what is unseen and eternal, above all the resurrection (v. 14).

== questions | 7
q: Why would God choose to put his treasure in fragile people instead of impressive ones? | So that the power is clearly his; our weakness points away from us to him.
q: Which of the four "but not" phrases in verses 8-9 speaks most to you, and why? | Answers will vary.
q: Paul calls his afflictions "light" and "but for a moment." How can he say that, given what he suffered? | He weighs them against eternal glory; compared with eternity, even great suffering is brief.
q: What practical things help you "look at the things which are not seen" when life is hard? | Scripture, prayer, worship with others, remembering the resurrection, encouragement from fellow believers.

== application | 3
Memorize 2 Corinthians 4:16-18 this week. When a trouble presses on you, say the "but not" out loud: "Pressed, but not crushed." Reach out to one person in the church who is going through a hard time, and share this passage with them.

== prayer | 2
Lord, we are clay jars, cracked and weak. Thank you for the treasure of the gospel you have placed in us. When we are pressed, keep us from being crushed; when we are cast down, keep us from despair. Renew us inwardly day by day, and fix our eyes on what is unseen and eternal, until we see you face to face. In Jesus' name, Amen.
