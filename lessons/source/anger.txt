== opening | 4
Ask: "What makes you angry?" Let people answer lightly (traffic, being kept waiting, injustice, being ignored). Then ask: "Is anger always wrong?" Let the class discuss briefly before reading. Many are surprised to find a verse that says, "Be ye angry."

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud.

== background | 5
Ephesians 4-6 turns from doctrine to daily life. Paul has just told believers to "put off ... the old man" and "put on the new man" (4:22-24). Now he shows what that looks like in speech, work and relationships. Each command has a reason attached: we are members of one another (v. 25); do not give the devil a foothold (v. 27); share with those in need (v. 28); do not grieve the Holy Spirit (v. 30); forgive as God forgave you (v. 32).

"Be ye angry, and sin not" quotes Psalm 4:4. Jesus himself was angry at hardness of heart (Mark 3:5) and at those who turned the temple into a marketplace (John 2:13-17).

== point | 7 | Anger must be handled quickly
There is a right anger, at injustice and sin, like Christ's own. But even right anger easily turns into sin. Paul gives a practical rule: "let not the sun go down upon your wrath." Anger that is kept overnight hardens into resentment, and that gives the devil a foothold.

== point | 7 | Put away bitterness and harmful words
Verses 29-31 list what has to go: corrupt speech, bitterness, wrath, anger, clamour (shouting), evil speaking and malice. In their place: words that build up and give grace to those who hear. Paul adds a tender warning: "grieve not the holy Spirit of God." Our unkind words do not just hurt people; they grieve God, who lives in us.

== point | 7 | Replace it with kindness and forgiveness
Paul does not only say "stop"; he says "start." "Be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you" (v. 32). The cure for bitterness is not just suppressing anger; it is remembering how much God has forgiven us, and passing that forgiveness on.

== questions | 7
q: What is the difference between righteous anger and sinful anger? | Righteous anger is directed at real wrong, seeks good, and is under control; sinful anger is selfish, harms others, and is nursed.
q: Why do you think Paul says not to let the sun go down on our anger? | Unresolved anger hardens into bitterness and resentment and gives the devil opportunity.
q: Which item in verse 31 is the hardest for you to put away? | Answers will vary.
q: How does remembering God's forgiveness help us forgive others? | We have been forgiven far more than anyone owes us (compare the parable in Matthew 18:21-35).

== application | 3
If you are carrying anger toward someone, settle it before the sun goes down: pray about it today, and take one step toward peace if it is safe and wise. Before you speak in a tense moment this week, ask: "Will this build up, or tear down?"

== prayer | 2
Lord, you know our tempers and our grudges. Forgive us for anger that has turned into sin, for bitter words and for the times we have grieved your Holy Spirit. Make us kind, tenderhearted and forgiving, as you have forgiven us for Christ's sake. Help us settle our anger before the sun goes down. Amen.
