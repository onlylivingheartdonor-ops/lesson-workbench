== opening | 4
Ask: "If you had to pick the most religious person in your town, who would it be, and why?" (Do not ask for names; ask what makes a person look religious.) Then explain that in Jesus' day the answer would have been a Pharisee, and the least religious would have been a tax collector. Jesus tells a story with both, and the ending surprised everyone.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. Have one reader read the Pharisee's prayer in a proud voice and another read the publican's prayer quietly.

== background | 5
Luke tells us exactly why Jesus told this parable: "unto certain which trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and despised others" (v. 9). Pharisees were respected religious leaders, devoted to the law. Publicans collected taxes for the Roman occupiers, often dishonestly, and were seen as traitors.

The Pharisee's claims were true. He fasted twice a week, more than the law required, and tithed everything. His problem was not his deeds but whom he trusted and how he looked at others.

== point | 7 | Pride trusts itself and looks down on others
The Pharisee "prayed thus with himself." His prayer was really a speech about himself, with a sideways glance at the sinner nearby. Pride uses others as a measuring stick, and always finds someone worse to stand above.

== point | 7 | Humility comes empty-handed
The publican stood far off, would not look up, beat his breast, and prayed seven words: "God be merciful to me a sinner." He offered no record, no excuses and no comparisons, only his need and God's mercy. Strangely, standing far off was the way to come near to God.

== point | 7 | God exalts the humble
"This man went down to his house justified rather than the other" (v. 14). The religious man went home unchanged; the sinner went home right with God. Jesus states the rule of his kingdom: everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted.

== questions | 7
q: What was good about the Pharisee's life? What was wrong with his prayer? | His disciplines were real; but he trusted them, thanked God mainly for himself, and despised others.
q: In what subtle ways do we compare ourselves with others to feel better? | Thinking "at least I'm not like ...", judging other churches, families, or generations.
q: Why is "God be merciful to me a sinner" such a powerful prayer? | It is honest, humble, and rests entirely on God's mercy; it is the doorway to forgiveness.
q: How can we hold high standards without falling into the Pharisee's pride? | Remembering that every good thing in us is by grace, measuring ourselves by Christ rather than by others, and staying aware of our own need.

== application | 3
Each day this week, pray the publican's prayer slowly and sincerely. When you catch yourself thinking "at least I'm not like them," stop and pray for that person instead.

== prayer | 2
God, be merciful to me a sinner. We confess that we have trusted in ourselves and looked down on others. Take away our pride, and give us humble hearts that rely only on your mercy. Thank you that you lift up the humble and receive all who come to you through Jesus Christ. Amen.
