== opening | 4
Ask: "What makes a worship service 'good' to you?" Answers might include the music, the sermon, the building or the people. Then ask: "What makes worship good to God?" This lesson looks at the most important conversation about worship in the Bible, which took place at a well, with a woman who had a complicated past.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. If there is time, read John 4:5-18 first so the class knows the setting.

== background | 5
Jesus was travelling through Samaria and sat by Jacob's well near the town of Sychar. Jews and Samaritans despised each other. The Samaritans were descendants of Israelites who had intermarried with foreign settlers after the Assyrian conquest. They accepted only the five books of Moses and worshipped on Mount Gerizim, while the Jews worshipped at the temple in Jerusalem.

Jesus had just shown the woman that he knew all about her life (vv. 16-18). When she said, "I perceive that thou art a prophet," she turned the conversation to the oldest religious argument between their peoples: which mountain is the right place to worship?

== point | 7 | Worship is not about the right mountain
The woman raised a question about location, perhaps to change an uncomfortable subject. Jesus took her question seriously, but he moved past it. "The hour cometh" when place would not matter. Arguments about worship can become a way of avoiding God himself.
verses: John 4:19-21; Acts 17:24-25; Malachi 1:11

== point | 7 | Worship in spirit and in truth
"God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth" (v. 24). Worship "in spirit" comes from the heart and is not merely outward ceremony. Worship "in truth" responds to God as he has really revealed himself. Both are needed: heart without truth becomes emotion, and truth without heart becomes empty form. The Father is seeking such worshippers.
verses: John 4:22-24; Psalm 51:16-17; Romans 12:1

== point | 7 | True worship centres on Jesus
The woman said, "I know that Messias cometh." Jesus answered, "I that speak unto thee am he." It is one of the clearest statements of who he is anywhere in the Gospels. True worship is not just a correct method; it is meeting the person of Jesus Christ. The woman went straight back to her town and brought others to him (vv. 28-30).
verses: John 4:25-26; John 14:6; Philippians 2:9-11

note: Christians worship in many styles: liturgical and informal, with hymns or with modern songs. This passage does not settle questions of style; it addresses the heart and the object of worship, which all Christians share.

== questions | 7
q: Why do you think the woman brought up the question about the right mountain? | Possibly to move away from her personal life; possibly a sincere question. Either way, Jesus brought the conversation back to God.
q: What does "in spirit" add to worship? What does "in truth" add? | Spirit: heart engagement, sincerity, the work of the Holy Spirit. Truth: worship shaped by who God really is, as revealed in Scripture and in Jesus.
q: What worship arguments do Christians have today, and how might Jesus' words help us? | Style, music, buildings, times. Jesus calls us beyond secondary matters to the heart and to himself.
q: What would it look like for you to worship "in spirit and in truth" this coming Sunday? | Answers will vary: preparing the heart beforehand, attending to the words sung, confession, attentive listening.

== application | 3
Before church this Sunday, spend ten minutes preparing your heart: confess sin, read a psalm, and ask God to help you worship in spirit and in truth. During the week, look for ways to worship God at home and work, since worship is not limited to one place.

== prayer | 2
Father, you are seeking true worshippers. Forgive us when our worship has been only outward, or when we have argued about worship instead of worshipping you. Give us hearts that worship in spirit and in truth. Thank you for revealing yourself in Jesus, the Messiah. May our whole lives be worship to you. Amen.
