== opening | 4
Ask: "What is the best gift you ever received that you did nothing to earn?" After a few answers, ask: "How did it feel to receive something you could not pay back?" Some people find gifts hard to accept; we like to earn things. Today's passage is about the greatest gift of all and why we cannot earn it.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. Ask the class to listen for the two words "But God" in verse 4, the turning point of the passage.

== background | 5
Paul wrote to believers in Ephesus, a large city in what is now Turkey and the home of the great temple of Diana (Artemis). Most of the church had been pagans. In chapter 1 Paul praised God for every spiritual blessing in Christ. In chapter 2 he reminds them where they started and what God did.

The passage moves in three steps: what we were (vv. 1-3), what God did (vv. 4-9), and what we are now for (v. 10). Paul includes himself ("we all", v. 3). Jew and Gentile, religious and irreligious alike, needed rescue.

== point | 7 | We were dead, and could not save ourselves
Paul does not say we were sick or weak; he says we were "dead in trespasses and sins" (v. 1). We were following the world's way, the devil's lead and our own desires. A dead person cannot help bring himself back to life. That hard truth is the background that makes grace good news.
verses: Ephesians 2:1-3; Romans 3:23; Romans 5:12

== point | 7 | But God, rich in mercy, made us alive
"But God" changes everything. Because of his great love, God made us alive together with Christ and raised us up with him. The reason is in God, not in us: he is "rich in mercy." Salvation is by grace, his undeserved favour, received through faith. It is "the gift of God," so no one can boast.

note: Christians understand the relationship between God's grace and our faith in somewhat different ways (for example, whether faith itself is the "gift" in verse 8). All agree on what this lesson teaches: we are saved by God's grace, not by our own merit, and receive it through faith.

== point | 7 | Saved for good works
Verse 10 is the other side of verses 8-9. We are not saved by good works, but we are saved for them. We are God's "workmanship." The Greek word gives us our word "poem": we are his handiwork, created in Christ for a life of good works that he has prepared. Good works are the fruit of grace, not the price of it.

== questions | 7
q: Why do you think Paul begins with such a dark picture (vv. 1-3) before the good news? | We cannot appreciate grace until we see our need; a rescue means most to those who know they were drowning.
q: What do the words "But God" (v. 4) mean to you personally? | Answers will vary; invite testimony of how God intervened.
q: Why does it matter that salvation leaves no room for boasting? | It keeps us humble, makes everyone equal at the foot of the cross, and gives all the glory to God.
q: How do verses 8-9 and verse 10 fit together? | Grace saves us apart from works; the same grace then produces good works in us. Works are the fruit, not the root.

== application | 3
Thank God every day this week, in your own words, for one specific thing his grace has done for you. Then look for one "good work prepared in advance" (v. 10): someone to help, encourage or serve. Do it as a thank-you to God, not to earn anything.

== prayer | 2
Merciful God, we were dead in our sins, but you loved us and made us alive in Christ. Thank you for grace we could never earn and a gift we could never repay. Keep us from boasting in anything but your mercy. Make us your workmanship, and lead us into the good works you have prepared for us. In Jesus' name, Amen.
