== opening | 4
Ask: "When you discover a great restaurant, a good doctor or a helpful book, what do you do?" (We tell people!) "Why is it often harder to tell people about Jesus?" Let the class answer honestly: fear, not knowing what to say, not wanting to seem pushy. Today we meet an unlikely evangelist who had none of the training and all of the enthusiasm.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. Briefly remind the class of verses 1-27: Jesus met a Samaritan woman at a well, knew all about her life, and told her he was the Messiah.

== background | 5
The woman had come to the well at noon, perhaps to avoid other women, because of her troubled past (vv. 16-18). She was a Samaritan, a woman, and someone with a reputation, three reasons people in that day would not have expected God to use her.

While she went to town, Jesus' disciples urged him to eat. He told them his food was to do the Father's will and finish his work, and he pointed to the fields "white already to harvest" (v. 35). As he spoke, the townspeople were probably coming out across those very fields to meet him.
note: The woman's "come, see" echoes Jesus' own invitation in John 1:39 and Philip's in John 1:46.

== point | 7 | Tell what Jesus has done
The woman left her water pot, so eager that she forgot her errand. Her message was simple: "Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did: is not this the Christ?" (v. 29). She did not preach a sermon or win an argument. She told what had happened to her, and invited others to see for themselves.

== point | 7 | The fields are white for harvest
The disciples thought harvest was months away; Jesus said it was now. "Lift up your eyes, and look on the fields; for they are white already to harvest" (v. 35). People around us are more ready to hear than we imagine. Some sow and others reap, and all rejoice together.

== point | 7 | From her word to their own faith
"Many of the Samaritans of that city believed on him for the saying of the woman" (v. 39). Then they asked Jesus to stay, and after two days many more believed. They told the woman, "Now we believe, not because of thy saying: for we have heard him ourselves." Our testimony is a doorway; the goal is for people to meet Jesus themselves.

== questions | 7
q: Why might God choose someone like this woman to be the first messenger to her town? | To show that his grace is for everyone and that the power is in Jesus, not in the messenger's credentials.
q: What makes "come and see" easier than trying to argue someone into faith? | It shares honestly, respects the other person, and points them to Jesus and his word rather than to our cleverness.
q: Who are the "fields white to harvest" around you? | Neighbours, co-workers, family, newcomers, people in crisis.
q: How can we help people move from hearing about Jesus to meeting him themselves? | Invite them to read the Gospels, to church or a study, to pray; live and speak in a way that points to him.

== application | 3
Write out your own "come and see" story in three or four sentences: what your life was like, how you came to know Jesus, and what difference he makes. Pray for one person by name this week, and look for a natural opportunity to share your story or to invite them to church.

== prayer | 2
Lord Jesus, you know everything we have ever done, and you love us still. Thank you for meeting us. Give us the eagerness of the woman at the well to tell others, "Come and see." Open our eyes to the fields white for harvest around us, and use our simple words to bring others to hear you for themselves. Amen.
