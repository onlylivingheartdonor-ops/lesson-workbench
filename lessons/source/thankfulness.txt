== opening | 4
Ask: "When did you last write a thank-you note? Who taught you to say thank you?" Then ask: "Why is it so easy to forget?" This lesson fits well around Thanksgiving, but the lesson of the ten lepers is for every week of the year.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. Before you read, tell the class to listen for how many were healed and how many came back.

== background | 5
Jesus was travelling toward Jerusalem along the border between Samaria and Galilee. Leprosy, a name used for several serious skin diseases, made a person ceremonially unclean. Lepers had to live outside towns and keep their distance from others (Leviticus 13:45-46). That is why the ten "stood afar off" and shouted.

The law of Moses required a healed leper to show himself to a priest, who would confirm the cure so the person could return to normal life (Leviticus 14). Jesus sent the ten off before they were healed, and they were cleansed "as they went." Nine kept going. One, a Samaritan, came back.
note: Jews and Samaritans did not usually mix (see John 4:9). Shared suffering had brought this group together, but it was the outsider, the Samaritan, who returned to give thanks.

== point | 7 | Ten cried for mercy and all ten were healed
All ten prayed, "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us." All ten obeyed by setting off for the priests. All ten were healed. Jesus' mercy was generous to every one of them, including the nine who never thanked him. God gives many good gifts to people who never acknowledge him (Matthew 5:45).
verses: Luke 17:11-14; Matthew 5:45; Psalm 145:9

== point | 7 | Only one returned to give thanks
"Where are the nine?" Barnes suggests they were busy doing what they had been told and eager to get on with their lives. They were not wicked, just forgetful. Ingratitude is often not rebellion but neglect. The one who came back turned around, praised God with a loud voice, and fell at Jesus' feet. Gratitude goes back to the Giver.

== point | 7 | Thankfulness receives the greater blessing
Jesus told the Samaritan, "Thy faith hath made thee whole." All ten were cleansed in body, but this man received something more: a personal encounter with Jesus and a word of welcome. Gratitude does not earn God's gifts; it brings us closer to the Giver. Those who seem furthest from God are sometimes the most thankful.

== questions | 7
q: Why do you think the nine did not come back? | Excitement, hurry to get home, assuming thanks could wait, or simply taking the gift for granted.
q: What blessings do we most easily take for granted? | Health, family, daily provision, freedom to worship, salvation itself.
q: Why might the outsider, the Samaritan, have been the one to return? | He may have been more aware that he had no claim on Jesus' mercy. Those who know they do not deserve a gift are often most grateful.
q: How does giving thanks change the person who gives it? | It turns attention from gifts to Giver, deepens faith, guards against pride and discontent, and brings joy.

== application | 3
Write three thank-you notes this week: one to God (a written prayer), one to someone in your family, and one to someone at church who has blessed you. Each evening, before bed, name three things God gave you that day.

== prayer | 2
Lord Jesus, you have had mercy on us again and again. Forgive us for the times we have gone on our way without turning back to thank you. Give us grateful hearts that see your gifts and praise you for them, in good times and hard times. Thank you most of all for the gift of yourself. Amen.
