== opening | 4
Ask: "What is the best piece of advice anyone ever gave you?" Let several people share. Point out that the book of Proverbs is written as a father's advice to his son. Today's chapter contains some of the most-loved verses in the Bible: "Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding."

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. Ask the class to notice the pattern of command and promise: "do this ... and God will do that."

== background | 5
Proverbs collects wise sayings, many from Solomon (1:1). Chapters 1-9 are longer speeches, each beginning "My son." Wisdom in Proverbs is not cleverness but skill in living well before God, and it begins with "the fear of the LORD" (1:7), a reverent trust in him.

Proverbs describes how life generally works in God's world; its promises are not guarantees for every circumstance. Verses 9-10, about full barns, state a real principle of God's blessing. But verses 11-12 immediately add that God's children also go through correction and hardship. The book of Job and the life of Jesus show that the righteous sometimes suffer.
note: Use verses 9-10 carefully. They teach generous, God-honouring use of money, not a formula for getting rich.

== point | 7 | Keep God's teaching close to your heart
The father urges his son not to forget his teaching, and to bind mercy and truth around his neck and write them on his heart (vv. 1-4). Wisdom is not just learned once; it is kept close, worn every day, and written inwardly until it shapes who we are.
verses: Proverbs 3:1-4; Deuteronomy 6:6-7; Colossians 3:16

== point | 7 | Trust God, not your own understanding
"Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths" (vv. 5-6). We need not stop thinking; we stop leaning on our own thinking as if it were the final word. To "acknowledge him in all thy ways" is to bring God into every decision.

== point | 7 | Honour God with your wealth and accept his correction
Wisdom reaches our wallets: "Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits" (v. 9). Giving God the first and best, not the leftovers, shows trust. Wisdom also reaches our hardships. God's discipline is not a sign that he has abandoned us but that he loves us as a father loves his son (vv. 11-12; Hebrews 12:5-11).

== questions | 7
q: What does it look like in practice to "lean not unto thine own understanding"? | Seeking God's word and counsel before deciding, being willing to be wrong, praying instead of just planning.
q: Why do you think conceit about our own wisdom is such an enemy of faith? | It makes God unnecessary; we stop listening, asking and trusting.
q: What is the difference between giving God the "firstfruits" and giving him what is left over? | Firstfruits is giving first, in trust, before we know if there will be enough; leftovers give God only what we did not need.
q: How can God's correction be a sign of his love? | A father who does not correct does not care; God's discipline shapes us into Christ's likeness.

== application | 3
Choose one decision you face this week. Before deciding, pray about it specifically, read a relevant Scripture, and ask a wise Christian for advice. Also review your giving: are you giving God the first, or what is left over?

== prayer | 2
Lord, we confess that we often lean on our own understanding. Teach us to trust you with all our hearts and to acknowledge you in all our ways. Help us honour you with our substance and receive your correction as the love of a Father. Direct our paths, through Jesus Christ, who is the wisdom of God. Amen.
