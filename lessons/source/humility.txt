== opening | 4
Ask: "Who is the most humble person you have known? What made them humble?" Most answers will describe someone who served others, was not concerned with credit, and was genuinely interested in other people. Point out that humility is less about thinking poorly of yourself and more about thinking of yourself less.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. Verses 6-11 may have been an early Christian hymn; consider reading them more slowly.

== background | 5
Philippians is a warm letter to a church Paul loved, but there were tensions (see 4:2). In chapter 2 Paul appeals for unity, and he knows that unity depends on humility. Then he gives the greatest example there is: Jesus Christ.

Verses 6-11 describe a downward path and an upward path. Christ, who was in the form of God, emptied himself, took the form of a servant, became a man and died on a cross. Then God highly exalted him, and every knee will bow to him.
note: "Made himself of no reputation" (literally "emptied himself") does not mean Jesus stopped being God. Christians agree that he remained fully God while becoming fully man; he set aside his heavenly glory and privileges and took the humble place of a servant.

== point | 7 | Look to the interests of others
Paul asks the church to be of one mind. To do that they must stop acting from rivalry or vanity and instead "esteem other better than themselves." Humility is not denying our gifts. It is refusing to let our own concerns crowd out everyone else's.
verses: Philippians 2:1-4; Romans 12:10; Galatians 5:13

== point | 7 | Have the mind of Christ, who humbled himself
"Let this mind be in you" (v. 5). Jesus did not cling to his rights. He took the lowest place, a servant and then a criminal's death on a cross. If the Son of God was willing to stoop that far for us, we can stoop to serve one another.

== point | 7 | God exalts the humble
The story does not end at the cross. "Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him" (v. 9). Every knee will bow, and every tongue will confess that Jesus Christ is Lord. The way up in God's kingdom is the way down. We can afford to be humble, because our honour is safe in God's hands.

== questions | 7
q: How would you define humility in your own words? | Answers will vary; aim for something like "putting others' interests alongside or ahead of our own, because we trust God with our honour."
q: Why is humility necessary for unity in a church or a family? | Pride and rivalry divide; humility makes room for others, listens, and yields where it can.
q: What does Jesus' example show us about what humility costs? | It cost him his glory, comfort and life. Humility often means giving up rights, credit or convenience.
q: How does knowing that God exalts the humble free us to serve without recognition? | We do not have to fight for honour; God sees and will reward in his time.

== application | 3
Do one act of service this week that no one will notice or thank you for. In a conversation, try to ask more questions than you answer and focus on the other person's concerns. Pray verse 5 each morning: "Let this mind be in me, which was also in Christ Jesus."

== prayer | 2
Lord Jesus, you were rich, yet for our sakes you became poor; you took the form of a servant and died on the cross. Forgive our pride and selfish ambition. Give us your mind, so that we look to the interests of others and serve one another in love. We bow before you and confess that you are Lord, to the glory of God the Father. Amen.
