== opening | 4
Ask: "Has anyone ever prayed for you during a hard time, and you knew it made a difference?" Let a few people share. Then ask: "Whom do you pray for regularly?" Today we watch Abraham pray for a whole city, and we learn what it means to intercede.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud, with readers for Abraham and the LORD.

== background | 5
In Genesis 18, three visitors came to Abraham's tent. One of them is identified as the LORD himself. After promising Abraham and Sarah a son, the LORD revealed his plan to judge Sodom and Gomorrah, whose wickedness was very great. Abraham's nephew Lot lived in Sodom.

God said, "Shall I hide from Abraham that thing which I do?" (18:17). Abraham is later called "the Friend of God" (James 2:23). Friends share their plans, and here God invited Abraham into a conversation about justice and mercy.
note: In the end, ten righteous people were not found, but God rescued Lot and his daughters (Genesis 19:15-16, 29). Genesis 19:29 says God "remembered Abraham," so Abraham's prayer was answered in a way he had not exactly asked.

== point | 7 | God shares his heart with his friends
Intercession begins with God. He told Abraham what he was about to do, and Abraham "stood yet before the LORD" (v. 22). Prayer for others grows out of knowing God and hearing his word. When we read Scripture and see the world through his eyes, we are moved to pray.
verses: Genesis 18:17-22; John 15:15; Amos 3:7

== point | 7 | Pray on the basis of God's character
Abraham did not appeal to his own goodness. He appealed to God's: "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?" (v. 25). He was confident that God would not treat the righteous like the wicked. The best intercession reminds God of who he is: just, merciful and faithful to his promises.
verses: Genesis 18:23-25; Exodus 32:11-14; Psalm 145:8-9

== point | 7 | Bold, humble and persistent
Abraham asked again and again: fifty, forty-five, forty, thirty, twenty, ten. He was bold enough to keep asking, and humble enough to say, "I ... am but dust and ashes" (v. 27). God never grew impatient; he agreed each time. Abraham cared about people who were far from God, and he did not give up.
verses: Genesis 18:26-33; Luke 18:1; 1 Timothy 2:1-4

== questions | 7
q: Why do you think God told Abraham about his plans for Sodom? | Because Abraham was his friend and covenant partner; God wanted to draw him into prayer and teach him about justice and mercy.
q: What do we learn from the way Abraham argued with God? | We may pray boldly and honestly, reasoning from God's own character, while remaining humble.
q: Abraham prayed for a wicked city. How does that challenge whom we pray for? | We should pray for people far from God, even those we disapprove of, not just friends and family.
q: How was Abraham's prayer answered, even though Sodom was destroyed? | God rescued Lot and his family because he "remembered Abraham" (19:29).

== application | 3
Make an intercession list this week: two family members, two friends, two people far from God, and your town or nation. Pray for each by name every day. Be specific, appeal to God's goodness, and do not give up.

== prayer | 2
Lord, thank you that you share your heart with your people and invite us to pray. Make us intercessors like Abraham: bold because you are good, humble because we are dust, and persistent because we care. We pray for our families, our friends, our neighbours and our nation. Judge of all the earth, have mercy, for Jesus' sake. Amen.
