== opening | 4
Ask: "If someone could prove that Jesus did not rise from the dead, what would be left of Christianity?" Some will say his teaching and his example. Then tell them Paul's surprising answer: without the resurrection, nothing is left. This lesson is ideal for Easter but belongs to every Sunday, since every Sunday celebrates the resurrection.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. Read verse 20 with extra emphasis.

== background | 5
Some in the Corinthian church were saying "there is no resurrection of the dead" (v. 12). Greek thinking often treated the body as a prison and hoped for the soul's escape from it; a bodily resurrection seemed crude or impossible. Paul answers in the longest discussion of resurrection in the Bible.

In verses 1-11 he summarizes the gospel and lists witnesses of the risen Jesus: Peter, the Twelve, more than five hundred at once (most of whom were still alive and could be asked), James, all the apostles and Paul himself. Then in verses 12-19 he follows the logic: if the dead do not rise, Christ did not rise either.

== point | 7 | If Christ is not raised, everything collapses
Paul is brutally honest. If Christ is not risen, preaching is empty, faith is empty, the apostles are liars, we are still in our sins, and believers who have died are lost (vv. 14-18). If our hope in Christ is for this life only, we are of all people most to be pitied. Christianity is not just good advice; it stands on an event in history.

== point | 7 | But now is Christ risen
Verse 20 turns the whole chapter: "But now is Christ risen from the dead." Paul has looked into the abyss of a world without the resurrection, and he steps back onto solid ground. The tomb was empty, and many witnesses saw the risen Lord. Everything Paul feared in verses 14-19 is reversed.

== point | 7 | The firstfruits: death is not the end
Christ is the "firstfruits of them that slept." The firstfruits were the first sheaf of the harvest, offered to God as a pledge that the whole harvest would follow. Jesus' resurrection guarantees ours. Death came through Adam; life comes through Christ. That is why believers who die are said to have "fallen asleep."

== questions | 7
q: Why does Paul say we would be "most miserable" if our hope in Christ were for this life only? | We would have given up much for a hope that was false, and believers who died would simply be gone.
q: Why does it matter that the resurrection was bodily, not just spiritual? | It means God redeems the whole person and the physical world; death is defeated, not just escaped.
q: What does "firstfruits" tell us about our own future? | Jesus' resurrection is the first part of the harvest that guarantees the rest; ours will follow.
q: How should the resurrection change the way Christians grieve? | We still grieve, but "not as others which have no hope" (1 Thessalonians 4:13).

== application | 3
Think of one fear or grief that the resurrection speaks to. Write 1 Corinthians 15:20 on a card and put it where you will see it daily. This week, tell someone why you believe Jesus rose from the dead.

== prayer | 2
Risen Lord Jesus, thank you that you are not in the grave. Because you live, our faith is not in vain and our sins are forgiven. Because you are the firstfruits, death is not the end for those who belong to you. Fill us with living hope and the courage to share it. Alleluia, Amen.
