== opening | 4
Ask: "Where were these words read the last time you heard them?" Many will say at a funeral. Ask: "Why do you think this passage is read at so many funerals?" These words were first spoken not at a graveside but at a supper table, the night before Jesus died, to frightened friends.
note: Be sensitive to anyone recently bereaved. This lesson can be a comfort, but allow people space.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud slowly.

== background | 5
John 13-17 records what Jesus said to his disciples in the upper room on the night he was betrayed. He had washed their feet, told them one of them would betray him, and said he was going where they could not follow yet (13:33-36). Peter would deny him. The disciples were shaken.

Into that fear Jesus spoke John 14. "Mansions" (v. 2) in the King James Version means dwelling places or rooms, not grand houses. The picture is of a father's large house with a place for every child.

== point | 7 | Let not your heart be troubled
Jesus begins with the disciples' hearts. He does not deny that they are afraid. He gives them something to hold on to: "ye believe in God, believe also in me." Faith in Jesus is the antidote to a troubled heart. That does not mean feelings disappear; it means we have a solid place to stand when they come.
verses: John 14:1; Psalm 46:1-3; Philippians 4:6-7

== point | 7 | A place prepared, and a promise to return
"In my Father's house are many mansions ... I go to prepare a place for you" (v. 2). Heaven is not vague; it is home, the Father's house, with room for all and a place for each. Best of all, Jesus promises to come again and receive us to himself: "that where I am, there ye may be also." The heart of heaven is being with Jesus.

note: Christians differ about some details of the end times, such as the order of events around Christ's return. All agree on what this passage teaches: Jesus will come again and bring his people to be with him.

== point | 7 | Jesus is the way
Thomas asked the honest question: "How can we know the way?" Jesus did not hand him a map; he pointed to himself. "I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me" (v. 6). The way to the Father's house is not a set of directions but a person to trust and follow.

== questions | 7
q: What troubled the disciples' hearts that night? What troubles hearts in our class today? | Jesus' leaving, betrayal, confusion, fear for the future; today: illness, grief, uncertainty, family worries.
q: What does the picture of a "Father's house" tell us about heaven? | It is home, personal, with belonging and welcome, and prepared by Jesus himself.
q: Why is "where I am, there ye may be also" the heart of the promise? | Heaven's greatest joy is being with Jesus, not simply the place.
q: Thomas admitted he did not understand. Why is honest questioning welcome with Jesus? | Jesus answered patiently; honest questions can lead to deeper faith.

== application | 3
Memorize John 14:1-3 this week. Think of someone who is grieving or anxious, and send them a note or make a call to share this promise. If you have unanswered questions about following Jesus, bring them to him in prayer, as Thomas did.

== prayer | 2
Lord Jesus, when our hearts are troubled, help us believe in you. Thank you for preparing a place for us in the Father's house, and for your promise to come again and take us to be with you. You are the way, the truth and the life. Keep us following you until we are home. Amen.
