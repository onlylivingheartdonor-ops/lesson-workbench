== opening | 4
Ask: "Think of a time you had to step into something new and frightening: a new job, a move, a diagnosis, a loss. What helped you take the first step?" Let several people share. Joshua stood at exactly that kind of moment.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. Ask the class to count how many times God says "be strong" or "be of good courage."

== background | 5
Moses was dead. For forty years he had led Israel out of Egypt and through the wilderness. Now Joshua, his assistant, had to lead a nation across the Jordan River into a land full of fortified cities. Joshua had been one of the two faithful spies forty years earlier (Numbers 14:6-9), so he knew both the promise and the danger.

God speaks to Joshua three times about courage in these nine verses. Notice what God does not do: he does not say the task will be easy. He gives Joshua his presence, his promise and his word.
note: The conquest of Canaan raises hard questions for many readers. This lesson focuses on what God says to Joshua personally about trusting him in a frightening new task.

== point | 7 | God's presence continues when leaders pass
"Moses my servant is dead; now therefore arise" (v. 2). The great leader was gone, but God was not. "As I was with Moses, so I will be with thee: I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee" (v. 5). Every generation faces losses. The same God who was with those before us is with us now.
verses: Joshua 1:1-5; Deuteronomy 31:8; Hebrews 13:5-6

== point | 7 | Courage is rooted in God's word
Joshua's courage was not to come from his own personality but from obeying God's word: "This book of the law shall not depart out of thy mouth; but thou shalt meditate therein day and night" (v. 8). Courage and obedience go together. When we know what God has said, we can step out even when we are afraid.

== point | 7 | Fear not, for God goes with you
Verse 9 is the summary: "Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest." Courage is not the absence of fear; it is moving forward in obedience because God is present.

== questions | 7
q: Why do you think God repeated "be strong and of a good courage" three times? | Joshua needed it; fear returns, and God patiently reassures us again and again.
q: What "Jordan River" are you facing, a new or frightening step? | Answers will vary.
q: How does knowing God's word give courage? | It tells us God's character and promises and shows us the right path, so we are not guided by fear.
q: Is courage the absence of fear? If not, what is it? | No; it is acting rightly in spite of fear, trusting God's presence.

== application | 3
Write Joshua 1:9 on a card and read it every morning this week. Name one step of obedience you have been afraid to take, and take it, trusting that God goes with you. Encourage someone else who is facing a hard new season.

== prayer | 2
Lord, you were with Moses and with Joshua, and you are with us. When we face new and frightening places, make us strong and of good courage. Keep your word in our mouths and hearts. Thank you for your promise never to fail or forsake us. Go with us wherever we go. In Jesus' name, Amen.
