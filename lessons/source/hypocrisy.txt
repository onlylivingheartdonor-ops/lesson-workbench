== opening | 4
Show (or describe) a polished apple with a bruise or worm hole on the inside. Ask: "What matters more about an apple, how it looks or what is inside?" Then ask: "Why are we so concerned with how we look to other people?" Today Jesus speaks some of his strongest words to the most respected religious people of his day.
note: Jesus' words here are severe. Make sure the class sees that the warning is for us and our churches, not just for "those Pharisees" long ago.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud.

== background | 5
Matthew 23 is the last public teaching of Jesus in Matthew. It includes seven "woes" against the scribes and Pharisees. The Pharisees were not villains in their own eyes; they were the most devout people around, serious about Scripture and holy living. Jesus even told people to heed their teaching (23:2-3). His charge was that they said but did not do, and did for show.

Barnes and Clarke both point out that "strain at a gnat" should read "strain out a gnat." Strict Jews poured their drink through a cloth so they would not swallow a tiny unclean insect. The camel was the largest unclean animal they knew. Tombs were whitewashed so people would see them and avoid touching them, which made a person ceremonially unclean (Numbers 19:16).

== point | 7 | Do not major on minors
The Pharisees carefully tithed even their garden herbs, but neglected "judgment, mercy, and faith." Jesus does not say small things do not matter: "these ought ye to have done." He says the weightier matters come first. It is possible to be careful about details and careless about justice, mercy and faithfulness.
verses: Matthew 23:23-24; Micah 6:8; Luke 11:42

== point | 7 | Clean the inside first
A cup polished on the outside but dirty inside is useless for drinking. The Pharisees washed carefully but were full of greed and self-indulgence within. Jesus' remedy is not more polish but a change of heart: "cleanse first that which is within." Only God can do that, by forgiveness and the new heart he gives.

== point | 7 | Do not be a whitewashed tomb
Whitewashed tombs look clean and bright, but inside they are full of dead men's bones. Jesus says some people "appear righteous unto men" while inside they are full of hypocrisy. God is not fooled by appearances. The good news is that he does not want us to stay that way. Honest confession is the opposite of hypocrisy.

== questions | 7
q: What "gnats" do churches sometimes strain out while swallowing "camels"? | Arguments over small matters of style or procedure while neglecting justice, mercy, faithfulness, or care for people.
q: Why is it so easy to care more about how we look than about our hearts? | We see others' opinions immediately; we fear exposure; image is easier to manage than inner change.
q: How can a church become a place where people do not need to pretend? | Leaders modelling honesty, confession, grace for failures, small groups where people can be known.
q: If only God can clean the inside, what is our part? | Honest confession, repentance, asking for his cleansing, and living by his Spirit.

== application | 3
Pray Psalm 139:23-24 every day this week: "Search me, O God, and know my heart." Ask God to show you one area where your outside and inside do not match. Confess it to him, and if it would help, to a trusted Christian friend.

== prayer | 2
Holy God, you see our hearts. Forgive us for caring more about how we look than about who we are. Forgive us for straining at gnats and neglecting justice, mercy and faith. Cleanse us within; create in us clean hearts. Make us honest before you and one another, through Jesus Christ, who washes us clean. Amen.
