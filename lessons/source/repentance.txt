== opening | 4
Ask: "Have you ever been lost, really lost, as a child or as an adult? What did it feel like to be found?" Let one or two people tell their stories. Explain that Luke 15 is the "lost and found" chapter of the Bible: a lost sheep, a lost coin and a lost son.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud, perhaps with a narrator, the younger son and the father. Today's reading stops at verse 24; the story of the older brother (vv. 25-32) can be read at home.

== background | 5
Luke 15 begins with the setting: tax collectors and sinners were coming to hear Jesus, and the Pharisees grumbled, "This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them" (15:2). Jesus answered with three parables. In each something is lost, searched for or longed for, found, and celebrated.

Asking for one's inheritance while the father was still alive was shocking; it was close to saying "I wish you were dead." Feeding pigs was the lowest job imaginable for a Jew, since pigs were unclean animals. Barnes notes that the younger son pictures the "publicans and sinners," and the older son the Pharisees and scribes.

== point | 7 | Sin promises freedom and leaves us starving
The younger son wanted his father's gifts without his father's presence. He found freedom, then famine. Sin always overpromises. By the end he was feeding pigs and wishing he could eat their food. Matthew Henry sums up the story in a few short sayings.
verses: Luke 15:11-16; Isaiah 55:2; Romans 6:23
quote: 24232 | It is bad, and the beginning of worse, when men look upon God's gifts as debts.
quote: 41179 | Shame, contempt, and distress are wedded to sin, and can never be divorced.
blank: The son wanted his father's gifts but not his father's ____. | presence
blank: Sin promises freedom but leads to ____. | famine (emptiness)

== point | 7 | Repentance is coming to our senses and coming home
"He came to himself" (v. 17). Sin is a kind of madness, and repentance is a return to sanity. The son saw his condition, remembered his father's goodness, decided to go back, and confessed honestly: "Father, I have sinned." Repentance is more than feeling sorry. It is a change of mind that turns our feet toward home.
verses: Luke 15:17-19; Psalm 51:3-4; Acts 3:19
quote: 41181 | A state of sin is represented in the sacred writings as a course of folly and madness; and repentance is represented as a restoration to sound sense.
blank: When he came to ____, he said, "I will arise and go to my father." | himself
blank: Repentance is a change of mind that ____ us toward God. | turns

== point | 7 | The Father runs to meet us
The most surprising person in the story is the father. He sees his son "a great way off," runs, hugs and kisses him, and interrupts his confession with a robe, a ring, shoes and a feast. That is Jesus' picture of God. We do not have to clean ourselves up before coming home. The Father is already watching the road.

== questions | 7
q: What do you think the younger son was really looking for when he left? | Independence, pleasure, freedom from his father's authority. He wanted the gifts without the giver.
q: What does "he came to himself" tell us about sin and about repentance? | Sin distorts our thinking; repentance is waking up to reality about ourselves and about God.
q: Why do you think the father ran? (In that culture, older men did not run.) | His love was greater than his dignity; Jesus shows God eager to welcome sinners home.
q: Is there anyone you know who is "a great way off" from God? How can we reflect the Father's welcome to them? | Answers will vary; pray for them, keep the door open, avoid the older brother's resentment.

== application | 3
Take time this week to "come to yourself." Ask God to show you any area where you have wandered from him, and confess it honestly. Then thank him that he runs to meet you. If someone you love is far from God, pray for them by name every day.

== prayer | 2
Father, like the son in the far country, we have wandered and wasted your gifts. Thank you that you watch the road for us and run to meet us when we turn toward home. Bring us to our senses where we have strayed. Clothe us in your grace, and teach us to welcome others as you have welcomed us. In Jesus' name, Amen.
