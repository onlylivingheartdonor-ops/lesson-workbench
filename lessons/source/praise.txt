== opening | 4
Ask: "Why do we forget good things so easily and remember hurts so well?" Let the class discuss for a minute. Then point out that David talks to himself at the start of this psalm. He tells his own soul to praise and not to forget. Praise is not always a feeling that comes over us; sometimes it is a choice we make.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud slowly. Consider having the whole class read verses 1-2 together as an opening call to worship.

== background | 5
Psalm 103 is titled "A Psalm of David." It is one of the best-loved psalms of praise and has been sung in churches for centuries, including in the hymn "Praise, My Soul, the King of Heaven." Matthew Henry says it "calls more for devotion than exposition."

The psalm moves outward. David begins with his own soul and the benefits he personally has received (vv. 1-5). Then he praises God's dealings with his people (vv. 6-13). At the end (vv. 19-22, beyond today's reading) he calls on angels and all creation to join in.
quote: 22078 | This psalm calls more for devotion than exposition; it is a most excellent psalm of praise, and of general use.

== point | 7 | Stir up your soul to praise
"Bless the LORD, O my soul" (v. 1). David speaks to himself. Praise involves "all that is within me": mind, heart and will. Sometimes we must remind ourselves to praise. Matthew Henry says that it is no foolishness to talk to yourself in this way when you are urging your own soul toward what is good.
verses: Psalm 103:1-2; Psalm 42:5; Psalm 146:1-2
quote: 22079 | David is here communing with his own heart, and he is no fool that thus talks to himself and excites his own soul to that which is good.
blank: Bless the LORD, O my ____. | soul
blank: Praise often begins as a ____, not a feeling. | choice

== point | 7 | Count his benefits
"Forget not all his benefits" (v. 2). David then names them: forgiveness, healing, redemption, loving-kindness, satisfaction, renewed strength (vv. 3-5). Adam Clarke says we should call them to mind and name the chief ones one by one. General thankfulness quickly fades. Specific remembering produces praise.
verses: Psalm 103:2-5; Psalm 116:12; Lamentations 3:22-23
quote: 33968 | Call them into recollection; particularize the chief of them
quote: 59563 | redeemeth--Cost is implied.
blank: Forget not all his ____. | benefits
blank: Praise grows when we remember ____ what God has done. | specifically
note: Verse 3, "who healeth all thy diseases," may raise questions from those who are ill or have lost loved ones. Christians agree that every healing, by medicine or by answered prayer, is God's gift and that final, complete healing comes in the resurrection. Christians differ on what to expect of miraculous healing today; that question belongs to a separate lesson.

== point | 7 | Praise God for who he is
In verses 8-13 David moves from what God has done to what God is like: merciful, gracious, slow to anger, plenteous in mercy. God does not deal with us as our sins deserve. He removes our sins as far as the east is from the west, and he pities his children as a father does. The deepest praise is not only "thank you for" but "you are."
verses: Psalm 103:8-13; Exodus 34:6-7; Micah 7:18-19
quote: 33976 | He has never apportioned our punishment to our sins, nor has he regulated the exercise of his mercy by our merits.
quote: 59567 | God's benevolence implies no merit.

== questions | 7
q: Why do you think David has to tell his own soul to praise? | Our hearts drift, forget and grow dull; praise sometimes begins as an act of the will.
q: Which of the benefits in verses 3-5 means most to you right now, and why? | Answers will vary.
q: What does "as far as the east is from the west" tell us about God's forgiveness? | It is complete and final; east and west never meet, so forgiven sin is never brought back against us.
q: How is praising God for who he is different from thanking him for what he has done? | Thanks responds to gifts; praise delights in the Giver himself, and continues even when circumstances are hard.

== application | 3
This week, keep a "benefits list." Each evening, write down three specific things God has done for you that day. On Sunday, read your list and pray Psalm 103:1-2 aloud.

== prayer | 2
Bless the Lord, O my soul, and all that is within me, bless his holy name. Lord, forgive us for forgetting your benefits. Thank you for forgiving our sins, redeeming our lives and crowning us with loving-kindness. You are merciful and gracious, slow to anger and plenteous in mercy. We praise you, through Jesus Christ our Lord, Amen.
