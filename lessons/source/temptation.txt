== opening | 4
Ask: "When are you most easily tempted? When you are tired? Hungry? Lonely? Stressed?" Many people know the saying about not making decisions when you are hungry, angry, lonely or tired. Today we see Jesus tempted when he was hungry and alone, and we learn how he stood firm.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud. Ask the class to count how many times Jesus says "It is written."

== background | 5
This event comes right after Jesus' baptism, when the Father's voice declared, "This is my beloved Son" (3:17). Immediately the Spirit led him into the wilderness, where he fasted forty days. The number recalls Israel's forty years in the wilderness. Where Israel often failed, Jesus, the true Son, stood firm. All three of his answers come from Deuteronomy 6-8, Moses' teaching to Israel about its wilderness years.

Matthew Henry calls this a "famous duel" between the Son of God and the serpent. Hebrews 4:15 tells us that Jesus "was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin."

== point | 7 | Temptation comes at our weak points
The devil came when Jesus was hungry and alone. He began with a doubt, "If thou be the Son of God," and a reasonable-sounding suggestion. Temptation rarely looks evil at first. It usually offers a shortcut to something good, by a path God has not given. Being tempted is not itself sin; Jesus was tempted and never sinned.

== point | 7 | Answer temptation with God's word
Each time, Jesus answered, "It is written." He did not argue or rely on his feelings; he stood on Scripture. When the devil quoted Scripture too (Psalm 91), Jesus answered with Scripture rightly understood. Putting God to the test by deliberately jumping into danger is presumption, not faith.

== point | 7 | Worship God alone, and resist
The last temptation was the boldest: all the kingdoms of the world in exchange for one act of worship. Jesus' answer was final: "Get thee hence, Satan." When we refuse to divide our worship, the tempter loses his hold. The devil left, and angels came and ministered to Jesus. God does not leave his people alone in the fight.

== questions | 7
q: Why do you think the devil began with "If thou be the Son of God"? | To plant doubt about Jesus' identity and the Father's word spoken at his baptism. Temptation often starts by questioning what God has said about us.
q: How did the devil misuse Scripture, and how do we guard against that? | He quoted Psalm 91 out of context to justify presumption. We guard against it by knowing the whole of Scripture and letting Scripture interpret Scripture.
q: What "shortcuts" are we tempted to take today to get good things in wrong ways? | Answers will vary: dishonesty at work, compromise in relationships, cutting corners on commitments.
q: How does knowing that Jesus was tempted help you when you are tempted? | He understands, he sympathizes, and he gives grace and a way of escape (Hebrews 4:15-16; 1 Corinthians 10:13).

== application | 3
Identify one temptation you face regularly. Find one verse of Scripture that speaks directly to it, write it down and memorize it this week. When the temptation comes, say the verse aloud and pray for strength.

== prayer | 2
Lord Jesus, you were tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin. When we are tired, hungry or alone, keep us from taking shortcuts. Fill our hearts with your word, so that we can answer temptation with "It is written." Help us worship and serve God alone, and give us your strength to stand. Amen.
