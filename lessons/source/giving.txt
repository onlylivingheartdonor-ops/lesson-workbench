== opening | 4
Ask: "What is the most joyful gift you ever gave someone?" Let a few people share. Then ask: "Why is it that giving can feel so good, and yet be so hard to do?" Today Paul writes about money, but his real subject is the heart.
note: Money can be a sensitive subject. Make it clear that the lesson is not a fundraising appeal and that no one's giving will be discussed.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud.

== background | 5
Chapters 8-9 of 2 Corinthians concern a collection Paul was organizing among the Gentile churches for poor believers in Jerusalem, who were suffering from famine and hardship. The Corinthians had promised to give a year earlier (9:2), and Paul encourages them to follow through.

Paul uses a farming picture throughout: sowing and reaping, seed and harvest. He also uses the word "grace" again and again for giving. Generosity is grace at work in a believer's life.

== point | 7 | Sow generously
"He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully" (v. 6). A farmer who hoards his seed will have a small harvest. Generous giving is like sowing. It is not lost; God brings a harvest from it, though the harvest is in his hands and may be spiritual more than material.
verses: 2 Corinthians 9:6; Proverbs 11:24-25; Luke 6:38

note: This passage is sometimes misused to promise that giving money will make the giver rich. Paul's point is that God supplies givers so that they can keep giving (v. 8, 11), and that the harvest includes righteousness and thanksgiving to God (vv. 10-12).

== point | 7 | Give willingly and cheerfully
"Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver" (v. 7). The word "cheerful" is related to our word "hilarious." Giving should be decided thoughtfully in the heart, not squeezed out by pressure, and done gladly.

== point | 7 | God supplies us so that we can give, and it ends in thanks
God "is able to make all grace abound toward you" (v. 8). He supplies seed to the sower. We are enriched "to all bountifulness" (v. 11), not so that we can keep it all, but so that we can share. Giving meets needs and produces thanksgiving to God. Behind it all stands the greatest gift of all: "Thanks be unto God for his unspeakable gift" (v. 15).

== questions | 7
q: Why do you think Paul uses farming pictures to talk about giving? | Sowing feels like losing seed but produces a harvest; giving is investment in God's work, not loss.
q: What makes giving "grudging" rather than "cheerful"? | Pressure, guilt, fear of not having enough, seeing it as a tax rather than worship.
q: What does verse 8 suggest about where our resources come from and what they are for? | They come from God, and he gives them so we can do good works and share.
q: How does remembering God's "unspeakable gift" change our attitude to giving? | God gave his own Son; our giving is a grateful response, not a payment.

== application | 3
Spend time this week praying about your giving: to your church, to people in need, to missions. Decide "in your heart" what God is leading you to give, and give it cheerfully. Look for one unexpected way to be generous: a meal, help with a bill, or your time.

== prayer | 2
Generous Father, every good gift comes from you, and you gave us your Son, your unspeakable gift. Forgive us when we have given grudgingly or not at all. Make us cheerful givers. Supply us so that we can give, and let our giving overflow in thanksgiving to you. In Jesus' name, Amen.
