== opening | 4
Ask married couples (or anyone with a long friendship): "What did you do when you were first in love that you do not do as much now?" Let people laugh and share. Then ask: "Does love grow cold suddenly, or slowly?" Today Jesus speaks to a church that was busy, faithful, and slowly cooling.

== scripture | 3
Read the passage aloud.

== background | 5
Revelation 2-3 contains seven short letters from the risen Jesus to seven churches in what is now western Turkey. Ephesus was the first and most important, a great port city with a famous temple of Diana. Paul had spent over two years there (Acts 19), and the church had good teachers, including Timothy.

Some forty years later, the church was still orthodox and hard-working. It tested false teachers and rejected the Nicolaitans (apparently a group teaching compromise with immorality). But something essential had slipped.
note: Barnes understands "the angel" of the church (v. 1) as its minister or leader; others take it as a heavenly messenger. Either way, the message is for the whole church: "hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches" (v. 7).

== point | 7 | Jesus knows our works
"I know thy works, and thy labour, and thy patience" (v. 2). Jesus noticed everything: their hard work, their endurance, their stand against false teaching. Nothing done for him is unseen. This is a comfort for every faithful, unnoticed servant.
verses: Revelation 2:1-3; Hebrews 6:10; 1 Corinthians 15:58

== point | 7 | Busy, but losing love
"Nevertheless I have somewhat against thee, because thou hast left thy first love" (v. 4). They had not lost correct belief or hard work; they had lost love: love for Christ, and probably love for one another. It is possible to keep doing all the right things while the heart slowly cools.
verses: Revelation 2:4; Matthew 24:12; Jeremiah 2:2

== point | 7 | Remember, repent, return
Jesus gives three steps (v. 5). Remember how it was when you first came to him. Repent: turn around. Return and "do the first works" again, the things you did when love was fresh. The warning is serious: a church without love may lose its lampstand. The promise is wonderful: the one who overcomes will eat of the tree of life (v. 7).
verses: Revelation 2:5-7; Hosea 14:1-4; Luke 15:20

== questions | 7
q: What did Jesus commend in the Ephesian church? Why do you think he started there? | Their work, endurance and discernment; he is fair and encouraging, and wants them to know he sees.
q: How can a church or a Christian be doctrinally sound and hard-working but still lose its first love? | Duty replaces devotion; routines continue while the heart drifts; truth is defended without love.
q: What were the "first works" in your early Christian life? | Answers will vary: eager prayer, reading Scripture, telling others, joyful worship, serving.
q: What practical steps help rekindle love for Christ? | Remembering his grace, confession, time in his word and prayer, worship, serving others in love.

== application | 3
Spend time this week remembering: write down how you came to faith and what Jesus meant to you then. Choose one "first work" to take up again. Pray each day: "Lord, restore my first love."

== prayer | 2
Lord Jesus, you know our works, and you know our hearts. Forgive us where our love for you and for one another has grown cold. Help us remember where we have fallen from, repent, and do the first works again. Rekindle our first love, and keep us faithful until we eat of the tree of life. Amen.
